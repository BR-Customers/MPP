About this Guide

The Shop Floor App is where operators create and move LOTs at the terminals. Each terminal shows a different screen depending on the location it is assigned to. This guide walks through every screen and explains what each one does.

Prepared by Blue Ridge Automation Draft v0.2 · 2026-08-25

How LOTs move

Every LOT that is not received at the Receiving Dock starts at Die Cast, where it is opened into a cavity and filled with parts. Where it goes next depends on its Route, which an administrator or supervisor defines in the Configuration Tool. A few LOTs are finished as soon as they leave Die Cast, but most move on to Trim or to Machining & Assembly, and some pass through all three. Between machines, LOTs wait in storage.

The three main stages of a LOT

1Die Cast

Where the parts in a LOT are first created. You scan the LTT barcode from the basket (this is what tracks the LOT along its route), pick the part, and assign the basket to a cavity. As parts are cast, output is recorded against the shift that produced it. Once the basket is full, it is released from its cavity.

2Trim

Some parts pass through here. On the Trim screen the LOT is checked in by scanning its LTT barcode. Once trimming is finished, the LOT is selected on the Trim OUT tab and released.

3Machining & Assembly

This stage has four screens: Machining IN, Machining OUT, Assembly IN, and Assembly OUT. Which of them a LOT passes through depends on its route. After leaving Trim, a LOT waits in the line's storage until it is picked into Machining & Assembly.

i
About LTT barcodes. An LTT barcode is 8 or 9 numeric digits. MPP's pre-printed label stock is 8 digits; 9-digit codes remain valid for LTTs that were already issued. LOTs that the system mints itself — machined sub-LOTs and finished goods, for example — get a generated name such as MESL1710935 instead.

Audits

Practically every action and event — Die Cast production, LOT movement, downtime entry — is recorded and stored in the database. Each log entry captures the operator who performed it, where and when it happened, and the data that changed. This is why operators sign in with their initials before using a terminal: it is what makes each action traceable to a person and a shift.

Other features

Beyond managing LOTs and machines, the Shop Floor App covers several other areas. Operators can enter their shift times and can start, end, and edit downtime events. Supervisors can obtain elevated access at a terminal in order to trace LOTs, manage holds, and search LOT details.

Login & Setup

The first time you use a terminal you are prompted to sign in. Signing in requires only your initials — the account system is not a security boundary, so there is no password. Its purpose is to record which operator performed which action. Everything operators do is audited and stored permanently, and can be reviewed later in the Audit Log in the Configuration Tool. If your initials are not on file yet, you are prompted to create a new user and to supply a Display Name. Initials must be unique, because initials — not the display name — are how each user is identified.

After signing in you land on the Terminal Entry page. Here you scan or select the location the terminal should be set to. If you do not have a barcode to scan, use the location tree below to find it. The tree follows the plant hierarchy: Enterprise → Site → Area → Line/Cell → Terminal.

Click the arrow to the right of any name to expand that branch, and keep going until you reach the terminal you want. Clicking the terminal's name sets your terminal to that location.

i
It is important to set your location correctly. Setting the terminal to a Die Cast, Trim, or Machining & Assembly location sends you to a different screen, and each Die Cast location only offers the parts that are eligible to be cast there.

The next few sections of this guide cover the individual locations and their screens.

Die Cast

Die Cast is the first stage of a LOT's life, and where the LOT is created. The screen has three tabs. On Open Basket you open an empty basket into each cavity of the mounted die. On Record Shift Output you record the shots produced during a shift and any scrap. On Lot Release you release a filled basket so it can move on to its next route step.

Opening baskets into a die

The Open Basket tab is a grid with one row per cavity of the die that is mounted on the cell. You fill in the rows you want to open and then open them all in one action.

  1. On a shared Die Cast terminal, pick the Active Cell first. Dedicated terminals already know their cell and do not show the picker.
  2. Check the Die shown above the grid. It is the tool currently mounted on the cell, and it determines which cavity rows appear.
  3. For each cavity you want to fill, choose the Part. Only parts that are eligible at this cell are offered. On a single-part die, fill in the first row and press Copy part to empty rows to apply it to every remaining empty row — each row stays editable afterwards.
  4. Scan the LTT Barcode into that cavity's row. The barcode is printed on the basket and is what identifies and tracks it along its route.
  5. Press OPEN BASKETS to open every completed row at once, or Clear to start over. Each row's Result column reports whether that basket opened successfully.
i
A cavity that already holds an open basket shows an Open pill with that basket's LOT name instead of an empty input. Release it on the Lot Release tab before opening another basket into the same cavity.

Recording shift output

Output is recorded against the shift that produced it. You enter one die-wide shot count and the system proposes how those shots divide across the open cavities; you then correct the good count and record any scrap per cavity. Shots this shift, good parts, and scrap are summarised on the right-hand side of the screen.

  1. Choose the shift in the Reporting shift dropdown.
  2. Enter the number of shots in Shots this entry (die-wide). This is the count for the whole die, not for one cavity.
  3. Press Compute / Preview. A breakdown row appears for each cavity. Rows for baskets that have already been released may also be listed for reference; those are not affected.
  4. Adjust the Good (pc) figure on any row that needs it, and press Add scrap reason to record scrap against that cavity. Each scrap line takes a defect code and a quantity, and you can add as many lines per cavity as you need.
  5. Press SUBMIT SHIFT OUTPUT. The tallies on the right update to match.

Scrap that cannot be attributed to a single cavity is recorded separately in the Shot loss (all cavities) row at the top of the tab: choose a Defect reason, enter the quantity, and press Register shot loss.

i
If a cavity's good count would push a basket past its capacity, the row shows an Over basket headroom warning and submitting opens a confirmation popup. For each over-filled basket you can either fill it — top it up to capacity, release it, scan a new LTT to open a replacement basket, and carry the remainder over automatically — or overfill it and continue past the limit. The headroom shown on a cavity row is a soft limit, not a hard stop.

Lot release

Once a basket is filled, release it on the Lot Release tab, which lists every basket currently open on this die. Each row offers two actions:

  • Release — frees the LOT from its cavity, moves it to the next step on its route, and opens the cavity back up. You may be prompted to deposit it into Trim Storage, where it waits until it is checked in to Trim. An under-filled LOT raises a warning, which you can accept if the short basket is intentional.
  • Void — shown only for empty baskets. This discards the LOT and stops it from moving along its route.

Trim

The Trim workflow is straightforward: you check a LOT in, and when trimming is finished you move it out and record any scrap.

On a shared Trim terminal, pick the press in the Active Cell dropdown first. On Check IN, scan or type the LTT barcode from the LOT. The LOT's details appear, along with whether it is eligible for Trim; the scan also enforces the cell's maximum-parts cap. LOTs already checked in at the press are listed under Currently in Trim.

The Trim OUT tab shows the same Trim inventory. Select a LOT by tapping its card, or scan its barcode, then record the good and scrap counts and release it. Released LOTs are deposited into the line's Trim Storage, where Machining IN picks them up.

Machining IN

Machining IN is where a LOT is claimed from Trim Storage and started at the line. You start a LOT by scanning its LTT.

  1. Scan or type the LTT into Scan LTT to start machining.
  2. Check the LOT name, part, and piece count shown in the review panel.
  3. Press Start Machining. The LOT moves from Trim Storage to this line and becomes the active machined LOT.

Press Inventory in the header to see what is currently held at the line, and Scrap to record rejects against a LOT.

Machining OUT

Machining OUT is where finished castings leave the machining line. It works from a FIFO queue of the castings pending at this cell, oldest first.

Select the parent LOT you want to extract from, then enter a piece count up to the parent's remaining count and choose a destination. Extracting fewer than the full count creates a sub-LOT: it is separated from its parent but stays linked to it in Genealogy (supervisor only). LTT labels print showing both the child and the parent LOT name. The parent stays open with its reduced count and can be extracted again until its remaining pieces reach zero, at which point it leaves the Machining OUT queue and its output moves into storage.

Assembly IN

Assembly IN is where component LOTs are brought to an assembly cell. Scan a LOT's LTT to check it in; the list shows the components currently at the cell.

  1. Scan or type the LTT into the Scan / enter LTT (LotName) field.
  2. Press Scan In. The LOT moves to this cell and joins Components at this cell.

Assembly OUT

Assembly OUT is where the finished good is built. It consumes the components standing at the cell and produces a finished-good LOT packed into a tray and container. There are two variants, and the terminal's configuration decides which one you see.

  • Non-serialized line — trays close by count, by weight from the line scale, or by the line camera, depending on the closure method configured for the part. Press Complete Tray to close the tray, then Complete on the Container Completion Gate to finish the container.
  • Serialized line — the same flow, with the vision station embedded in the screen and the PLC closing the container once the target weight is reached.

Both variants show the part being produced, the components available at the cell, and a live projection of how many finished goods the standing components can support — components running low are flagged. CRT Validation in the header opens the container-validation queue for parts that require it.

Obtaining Supervisor Access (Elevated Status)

Supervisors who need to trace LOTs, manage holds, or run inspections must sign in with their own credentials. Press Supervisor Access in the header of any page and enter your Active Directory credentials when prompted.

If you cannot sign in and you are certain the username and password are correct, you may not be registered as a user in Active Directory.

Supervisor menu

The menu button at the top left of the header opens the Supervisor Menu. From here you can reach every screen in the project and switch terminals using the location tree on the left. Many of these screens are available only to users with elevated status.

!
Selecting a terminal in the location tree removes your elevated status. You must sign in again to keep using the Supervisor Menu. Elevated status also expires after a period of inactivity.

LOT Search

i
LOT traceability features are available only to users with elevated access.

LOT Search is a cross-area lookup over every LOT in the system. Narrow the list by query, status, and origin, then click a row to open that LOT's details.

Filtering

Filter optionInput type / how to use it
QueryText field — the LOT name (LTT barcode or minted name), the vendor LOT, or the part number.
Status filterDropdown — the LOT's current status: Good, Open, Hold, Scrap, or Closed. Leave it on Any to include all of them.
OriginDropdown — where the LOT came from: manufactured on site, or received. Leave it on Any to include both.

Press Search to apply the filters, or Reset to clear them and start over.

LOT Details

Clicking a row opens the LOT Detail page. A strip across the top shows the LOT name, item, piece count, current location, tool, cavity, and total scrap, along with status, hold, and CRT indicators. Eight tabs below it cover the rest.

Tab overview

History

Traces everywhere this LOT has been. Each row carries a timestamp and the operator responsible. It logs movements (Die Cast to Trim, for example), status changes (open to closed), contributions (pieces added), and more.

Genealogy

Shows this LOT's parents and children. A sub-LOT extracted at Machining OUT is a child; the casting it came from is the parent.

Paused-at

Lists any open pause rows against this LOT — points where it was stopped part-way through a step and is waiting to resume. A LOT that was never paused shows an empty state here.

Linked Container

For a finished good, shows the container and tray it is packed into and the AIM Shipper ID it shipped under. A LOT that is not packed into a container says so; finished goods link to their container at Assembly OUT.

Inspections

Every inspection recorded against this LOT. Inspections are captured at the Inspection terminal — the Inspections button in the action bar goes straight there. The part also needs Quality Specs configured in the Configuration Tool, since those define what is inspected.

Hold

Place a hold on this LOT, or release the hold that is on it, without leaving the detail page. This is the same panel used on the Hold Management screen.

Scrap

Record scrap directly against this LOT, with a defect code, a quantity, and optional remarks.

Correct Count

Adjust the LOT's piece count when the recorded quantity does not match what is physically in the basket. The correction is audited like any other change.

The action bar at the bottom of the screen holds Back to Home, LOT Search, Reprint LTT, Inspections, and a button that sets or clears the LOT's CRT flag.

Genealogy Lookup

i
LOT traceability features are available only to users with elevated access.

Genealogy is the parent–child relationship between LOTs. This screen renders it as a tree: the ancestors the searched LOT descends from, and the descendants that came out of it. It is the query surface used to answer Honda traceability questions.

Enter the LOT name in the LotName field, choose whether to walk up, down, or both in the Direction dropdown, and press Walk Tree. Ancestors are listed above the searched LOT and descendants below it, with the LOT you searched for highlighted between them.

Track & Trace

i
LOT traceability features are available only to users with elevated access.

Track & Trace is a global lookup that resolves almost any identifier back to a LOT and then shows the path that LOT has taken. Use it to review the production events behind a LOT and who recorded them.

Scan or type any LOT name, serial number, or container identifier into the Identifier field and press Search. If the identifier resolves to more than one LOT, pick the right one from the candidate list. You then get that LOT's summary, its history timeline, and its production events. For a fuller view of parent and child relationships, use the Genealogy Lookup.

Downtime

The Downtime popup is where machine downtime is managed. You can start a new downtime, edit an existing one, and add downtime that has already happened. Open it with the blue Downtime button in the header.

A downtime event has the following properties:

  • Reason — categorises the downtime, which makes past events easier to sort and report on. Supervisors create new reasons in the Configuration Tool.
  • Start date and End date — when the downtime ran. Both can be set by hand, or filled in automatically when you press the start and end buttons.
  • Duration — for downtime that has already happened and whose exact start and end are not known, switch the editor to Duration only and enter how long the machine was down. Events entered this way are flagged as approximate.
  • Remarks — optional. Extra context explaining the downtime, which is useful when reviewing past events.

Pressing Start Downtime creates a new event with the start time set to now; you can set the reason afterwards. In the list, End appears on the active event and sets its end time to now. Edit lets you change the properties by hand. Void deprecates the event — the data is not deleted, but the event no longer counts and cannot be edited further.

Downtime is organised by shift. Use the dropdown at the top of the popup to change shift, which is also how you record a downtime event against an earlier shift.

Holds

A hold stops a LOT from being moved or consumed. When a hold is placed, the LOT's status badge updates to show it and the terminals refuse to act on it until the hold is released.

A hold can be placed on a LOT at any location. Common reasons include a failed quality inspection or a customer complaint. The hold stays on the LOT until a supervisor releases it.

Hold Management

i
This screen is available only to users with elevated access.

The Hold Management screen is where supervisors view, place, and release holds on LOTs and containers. It has two tabs: Open Holds and Bulk & Scrap.

Open Holds

The left column lists every open hold. Narrow it with the Filter field, which matches LOT name, AIM Shipper ID, item, or location, and with the Hold Type dropdown. Select a row to load it into the panel on the right, or press New hold to start from an empty panel.

Placing a hold

  1. Identify the target: enter a LOT name (scan the LTT) or a Container Id, then press Load.
  2. Choose a Hold type. This is the broad category the hold falls under, and it is what the Hold Type filter sorts on later.
  3. Type a Reason. Be specific — this is what the next person reads when deciding whether the hold can come off.
  4. Press Place hold.

Releasing a hold

With a held LOT or container loaded, the Release hold group shows a summary of the current hold. Enter Release remarks explaining why it is being released, then press Release hold. A LOT on hold can also be released from the Hold tab of its LOT Detail page.

Bulk hold

On the Bulk & Scrap tab, Bulk Hold places one hold across several LOTs at once. Search for LOTs by name or part, select the ones you want, choose a hold type, add an optional reason, and place the hold on all of them together.

Scrapping a held LOT

The same tab has a Scrap Held LOT panel for recording scrap directly against a LOT that is on hold — no split, and the hold stays in place. Enter the LOT name, the reject quantity, a defect code, and any remarks, then press Record Scrap.
