Tablet selection for the die cast cells

Three devices, evaluated for one job: running the MES operator interface at a die cast machine, all shift, every shift. Because the MES runs in the Ignition Perspective app and all processing happens on the gateway, conventional tablet specs — processor, memory, storage — have almost no bearing on this decision. Ambient temperature does. This document lays out the three candidates against that constraint and the handful of others that actually matter.

The deciding constraint

Rated operating range vs. the die cast floor

Every candidate runs the MES software identically. What separates them is whether the device is rated to operate in the air temperature surrounding a die cast machine in August.

Read the overlap. The iPad's rated ceiling falls inside the expected ambient band, not above it. Apple specifies iPad for ambient temperatures between 32°F and 95°F and states that use in hot conditions can permanently shorten battery life; above that ceiling the device throttles, then displays a temperature warning, then becomes unusable until it cools. Both rugged candidates clear the band with margin. The shaded band is an estimate based on typical die cast enclosures and has not yet been measured at MPP — confirming it is the first action item in this document.

Not recommended for the cell

Fine for the August FAT in a conditioned conference room. Not rated for sustained use at the machine, and no gloved-touch support.

Recommended

Clears the expected band, supports gloved input natively, and offers an integrated barcode scanner. The default choice unless measurement says otherwise.

Maximum margin

Choose if measured ambient runs above roughly 110°F sustained, or if cells see significant radiant load from the machine. Brighter display and longer warranty included.

Specify the G2 generation, and compare what is in the box

Getac ZX10 units listed on general industrial-supply sites are frequently the prior generation, identifiable by a Qualcomm Snapdragon 660 processor and 4 GB of RAM. The current generation is the ZX10 G2 — Qualcomm QCS6490, 8 GB RAM, with substantially more remaining security-update life. Purchase orders should name the G2 explicitly.

The headline price gap is also narrower than it first appears. The Zebra figure is the bare tablet: its power supply and the OneCare contract required for OS and security updates are separate line items, bringing a deployed unit to roughly $1,250. The Getac figure already includes the AC adapter, power cord, two batteries, and a three-year Bumper-to-Bumper warranty covering accidental damage. Compared on equal terms the difference is closer to $400 per unit than $700, and the Getac arrives with damage coverage the Zebra does not have at any price.

Scope clarification

What does not drive this decision

MPP's original inquiry asked for minimum processor, memory, and storage specifications. For this application there aren't any worth stating, and here is why.

The tablet is a display, not a computer

Ignition Perspective renders in the Perspective app while all logic, database access, and historical queries execute on the Ignition gateway. The tablet receives screen updates over a network connection. Any current tablet performs identically.

No MES data is stored on the device

Production records, lot data, and downtime events are written to the gateway and its database. Storage capacity on the tablet is irrelevant, and a lost or damaged tablet results in no data loss.

Screen size matters, resolution largely does not

The die cast operator screen is being designed against a 10-inch landscape viewport. All three candidates are in that class. Blue Ridge asks that the selected model be fixed before FAT so the interface is validated at its production dimensions.

Network reliability matters more than any spec

The Perspective app holds a continuous connection to the gateway. Wireless coverage at each intended tablet position will affect the operator experience more than any hardware difference in this table.

Decision required

How the tablet lives at the machine

This choice drives the accessory list, the per-station cost, and how operators scan. It needs to be settled before hardware is ordered. Blue Ridge's recommendation is noted, but the right answer depends on how far an operator must travel from the machine to complete a scan.

Option A

Handheld with charging cradle

Operator picks the tablet up, carries it to the die, rack, or staging area, and returns it to a cradle at the station.

Best when
Scanning happens away from the machine control station.
Trade-off
Highest breakage exposure and the only option where battery wear and dead-tablet- at-shift-change are real concerns. In a hot cell, battery life degrades fastest here.

Multiply the selected option by the number of die cast cells in scope, then add one spare unit per cell area. The spare matters more than it appears: without one, a damaged tablet means a machine running without MES coverage until a replacement arrives.

Two cost items that are easy to miss

The Zebra charge cradle ships as a bare cradle — the power supply, DC line cord, and AC line cord are three separate part numbers that must be ordered with it, or the cradle arrives unusable. And OneCare is not an optional extended warranty: without an active contract, Zebra does not provide operating system updates or security patches. Both should be on the purchase order from the start rather than discovered later.

Beyond the device

Additional considerations

Items MPP should decide on alongside the hardware. Several affect the interface design and are needed before FAT.

Barcode scanning method

Camera scanning needs no extra hardware but opens a full-screen camera for each scan and depends on clean labels and good lighting. An integrated scan engine gives operators a physical trigger button and reads reliably through soot and release-agent film. Blue Ridge recommends the integrated scanner if scanning happens more than a few times per shift. This choice affects how the screens are built, so it should be settled before FAT.

Gloves

MPP should confirm which gloves die cast operators wear. Standard capacitive touchscreens, including the iPad's, do not respond through most work gloves. Both rugged candidates accept gloved input directly.

Mounting and power

Blue Ridge recommends a charging dock at each machine rather than a central charging station, so tablets have a known home position and are always charged. If tablets stay docked and powered, the Zebra battery-free configuration removes battery replacement from the maintenance picture entirely.

Device management

A mobile device management platform lets MPP IT push the gateway address and MES project to every tablet at enrollment, enforce passcodes, remotely wipe a lost unit, and lock each tablet to the MES app so operators cannot exit to a browser or app store. Recommended for production; not required for a conference-room FAT. This is an MPP IT decision, and Blue Ridge asks that IT be included from this point forward.

Wireless coverage and network path

Before FAT, confirm that the conference room network can reach the Ignition gateway. Guest wireless and segmented VLANs frequently cannot, and this is the most common cause of a delayed FAT start. For production, confirm coverage at each machine position.

Case and camera interference

If camera scanning is selected, whatever case or boot is fitted must leave the rear camera fully exposed and unrecessed. Rugged cases frequently shroud the camera enough to prevent the barcode from focusing, which is a difficult problem to diagnose after deployment. The accessory list in the previous section notes which items apply to each mounting configuration.

Important distinction

FAT hardware should be production hardware

August FAT is being held in a conference room. In that environment all three candidates will perform flawlessly, which means FAT will validate the MES workflows but will prove nothing about temperature, gloves, scanning through dirty labels, or wireless coverage at the machine.

The risk of testing on different hardware

If FAT is conducted on consumer tablets and production deploys a different device, every layout, scanning, and ergonomic issue is discovered during commissioning instead of during acceptance. Blue Ridge strongly recommends that FAT units be the same models destined for the floor, so that acceptance testing carries forward.

Blue Ridge recommendation

Proposed path forward

In order. Steps 1 and 2 should begin now to keep the August FAT date.

  1. Measure the actual ambient temperature at a die cast cell.

    Place a temperature data logger at the intended tablet position on one cell for one week. This converts the central question of this document from an estimate into a number, and it is inexpensive. Below roughly 110°F sustained, the Zebra ET401 is sufficient. Consistently above that, specify the Getac ZX10 G2.

  2. Decide the barcode scanning method.

    Camera or integrated scan engine. Blue Ridge needs this to finalize the die cast operator screen. Please also confirm what will be scanned and roughly how often per shift.

  3. Choose the mounting configuration.

    Handheld, fixed mount, or quick-release hybrid. Blue Ridge recommends the fixed mount where the workflow allows, because it removes charging, battery wear, and drops from the picture at the lowest per-station cost. This decision and the scanning decision are linked, and together they determine the accessory purchase order.

  4. Order three units of the selected model for FAT.

    Two allow concurrent multi-station testing, which is required to validate that two operators working different machines do not conflict. The third is for the Blue Ridge team to drive the session. These should be production-intent units, not loaners.

  5. Bring MPP IT into the discussion.

    For device management, wireless coverage at the cells, and the network path from the FAT conference room to the Ignition gateway. Confirm and test that path at least one week before FAT.

  6. Plan an on-machine validation at commissioning.

    Regardless of device selected, scanning performance, glove usability, screen legibility under plant lighting, and thermal behavior should be verified at an actual cell before go-live. Blue Ridge will include this in the commissioning plan.

If MPP elects to proceed with iPads

Accepted-risk conditions

The iPad is a capable device and will run the MES correctly. If MPP prefers it for cost, familiarity, or fleet-standardization reasons, Blue Ridge asks that the following be acknowledged so that expectations are aligned:

Thermal behavior is expected, not a defect

Temperature warnings and shutdowns at the cell during warm months are consistent with the manufacturer's published specification and are not an MES fault.

The price gap is smaller than it appears

The headline difference is $349 against $926. But a deployed station needs a mount or cradle, power, a protective case, and a screen protector regardless of which tablet is chosen, and those costs are similar across all three. The gap narrows further once the iPad's shorter service life is counted: sustained operation above the rated ceiling permanently reduces battery capacity, so MPP should budget a materially shorter refresh interval than the 8-year supported life of the rugged alternatives.

Gloves must be addressed separately

Either touchscreen-compatible gloves are issued, or operators de-glove to use the tablet. The second option tends not to hold up in practice.

Procure through Apple Business Manager

Units bought through an authorized reseller enrolled in Apple Business Manager can be automatically enrolled and supervised by MPP IT. Units bought at retail generally cannot, and correcting that later means wiping and reconfiguring every device by hand.
