The asset register

MPP’s fixed-asset register for tooling. Two sheets, four columns each — Tag #, Description, VENDOR, DATE.

SheetTags allocatedPopulatedRange
Die Mold210170DM0001–DM0210
Trim Die6328TD0001–TD0063
 The trailing empty rows are pre-allocated tag numbers reserved for future purchases, not missing data.

DATE is the acquisition date, not a service date. The series opens 2019-01-28 and runs to 2026-08-20 at a steady 20–28 dies a year.

The Description is an implicit three-part key:

  59B      C/H #2,3,4      die "K"
<family>   <component>   <generation>

The generation letter carries the weight. Dies are replaced alphabetically, so 59B/5PA Fuel Pump walks L → M → N … → W across DM0001…DM0092. It is the only thing separating one physical die from its predecessor.

Where the register is soft

  • Vendors are free text — 20 spellings for 8 real vendors (Accu-Die / Accudie / Accu Die / Accu- die / Accudie-Die).
  • Two trim dies sit on the Die Mold sheet — DM0087 and DM0088.
  • Descriptions decay over time. Early rows are complete (59B C/H IN/EX 1,5 die G); later ones drop the component, the family, or both. DM0108 reads 2,3,4 M. DM0132 reads 64A G. Around 44 rows need restating before the register can stand as a system of record.

Structure

A row is a part, not a die

A die appears once per part number it casts. Everything confusing about this report follows from that one fact.

Take the 59B cam holder, generation K. The report prints die K ten times under ten part numbers — but there are two shot counts hiding in those ten rows, and therefore two physical dies:

The part numbers in each cluster are that die’s cavities — one shot fills all of them at once. Which is why the counts inside a cluster are near-identical but not equal: part 189 shows 216,434 where its siblings show 216,057. The legacy system increments a counter per (part, die) row, not per die, and the rows have drifted a few hundred counts apart.

Consequence

The two figures at the bottom of the report count rows, not dies. “Total Dies in Mass Production: 103” is 103 part-die rows — the real number is 76 dies. Collapsing the whole report gives 224 physical dies, not 343.

The status column

What C / B / W / A means

It belongs to the die, not the part

Of 224 physical dies, exactly one has its parts disagreeing about status — part 656-090, die B, flagged A while the three other parts cast by that same die read W. That is the entire population of “Approved” in the report. Across every part-die pair in the file, there are zero genuine conflicts. So a die has one status, printed once per part it makes.

“Current” is not exclusive

82 parts have exactly one Current die — but six do not. 152-090 and 143-090 (the 59B/5PA fuel pump) each carry five Current dies, 155-090 four, 624-090 three. Current means “in the production rotation”, not “the one that is mounted”. Sixteen parts have no Current die at all — PGE, P8A, RDJ, RNA, R70, RNO, the Aisin water pump, and the new Astemo / 6VJ / 6MD programs, which are all still Waiting.

 Every generation is a pair — a 4-cavity die covering journals 1 and 5, and a 6-cavity die covering 2, 3 and 4. G and O are in the register but not yet (or no longer) in the shots report. The tick on each bar marks the 150,000 planned life.

What “Waiting” actually gates

It is the shot life that is awaiting approval, not the die. Every single Current die (76 of 76) and every single Backup die (52 of 52) has a planned shot life set. Of the 96 Waiting dies, 85 have no shot life at all — the column reads 0.

So the sequence is: the die is bought, it runs, and at some point its planned life is signed off and it enters the Current/Backup rotation. Until that signature it shows as Waiting with a zero limit — which is also why the report paints it red.

The eleven exceptions prove it from the other side: 143-090 dies T and U, 648-090 dies C and E, 125-090/126-090 die G and a few others are Waiting with a life set — dies that had been approved and have since been stood down.

The red flag, and why 227 is wrong

The report paints the Shot Life Planned cell red on some rows. The rule is exact — it reproduces all 343 rows with no mismatches:

red  ⇔  Status <> 'B'   AND   GoodShots > ShotLifePlanned

Two things are buried in that. It tests Good shots, not Total — so a die past its life on total shots but under it on good ones goes unflagged. And it silently exempts Backup dies.

The footer reports 227 dies over planned life. At the physical-die level that is 130 dies, and they are not the same thing:

84 have no approved limit at all 46 are genuinely past an approved life
84 — no limit set 46 — truly over

Of the 46 genuinely over their limit, 39 are Current dies still in the rotation.

The number is wrong in both directions

It over-counts, because 84 of the 130 flagged dies have no approved limit to exceed — the flag says nothing about wear.

And it under-counts, because the rule exempts Backup: 45 of the 52 Backup dies are past their approved life and are never shown as such. Those are the dies MPP would fall back on.

Where the two disagree

Dies with no asset number

The two documents share no identifier — the only key is the human-readable description. Matching on family, component and generation letter, then splitting die sets by journal position and cam bank, joins 138 of 198 register rows to a shot record. Thirty of the sixty that fail are trim dies, which a die-cast shots report legitimately does not cover.

The part that matters runs the other way: 55 dies appear in the shots report with no MPP asset number at all, and they cluster by family rather than scattering — 5A2, 5K8, 5LA, 5MH, RXO, RNA, RDJ, RNO, R1A, R5A, R70, SZAX, PGE, P8A. Mostly Honda Anna and other-customer parts, and several are Current dies with 200,000+ shots on them.

Either those dies are customer-owned and MPP never capitalised them, or they predate the register’s 2019 start. The first fits better — 5A2 Fuel Pump die T is a recent Current die and still untagged — but neither document settles it.

Open

What the documents cannot tell us

  1. Are the untagged families customer-owned, or simply older than the register?
  2. True cavity count for single-part dies. The report reveals only distinct part numbers cast per shot, so a die with four identical cavities making one part number reads as one cavity. DM0170 5GO N die 4 cavity says so in its own description and still comes out as 1.
  3. Is a zero Shot Life Planned always “not yet approved”, or is it sometimes deliberate?
  4. Confirmation of the ~44 restated register descriptions.
